This is a complete guide to the WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) in 2025.

If you’ve just started delving into your web accessibility compliance journey, you will no doubt have come across mentions of WCAG.

WCAG is what all global disability acts are based on when it comes to web accessibility, which means it’s essential to have an in-depth understanding of these requirements. Doing so can enhance the inclusivity of your site and help you avoid lawsuits and fines, which start from $55,000 to $150,000.

Key Takeaways
Global disability acts like the ADA, AODA, and the EAA refer to WCAG when outlining their web accessibility standards and requirements.
WCAG is based on four core principles. Content must be Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust.
There are three different compliance levels associated with WCAG, namely Level A, AA, and AAA. Level AA is what most businesses should strive to achieve.
The latest version of the guidelines is WCAG 2.2.
Accessibility is a legal requirement in many regions and failure to adhere to the WCAG guidelines can result in costly fines, lawsuits, and brand damage.
