It's been years since the main *[Harry Potter](https://www.cbr.com/tag/harry-potter/)* series concluded, but the nostalgia of the saga still spawns theories about some of its most important moments. As every fan knows, Harry survived the killing curse as a baby because his mother's love protected him. However, after Voldemort returned using Harry's blood to regain his full body, he was allowed to touch Harry without suffering negative consequences. This raises the question of how Harry survived the killing curse a *second* time in *Deathly Hallows*.

Being able to touch Harry would, theoretically, mean Voldemort could kill him, hinting that something else was at play. Amid [a few online misconceptions](https://en.m.wikibooks.org/wiki/Muggles%27_Guide_to_Harry_Potter/Books/Deathly_Hallows/Analysis#:~:text=Voldemort%20unwittingly%20makes%20himself%20a,life%2C%20therefore%20Harry%20will%20live), two of the main theories can be discussed and refuted to really understand how it happened. The first is that the Resurrection Stone brought Harry back after Voldemort cast Avada Kedavra. The second is that when Voldemort restored his body using Harry's blood in *The Goblet of Fire*, he became a Horcrux of Harry. However, these theories are easy to refute, or at least challenge, and the true explanation is much more straightforward.

Harry discovers he's one of Lord Voldemort's Horcruxes [through Severus Snape's memories](http://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-movies-snape-scenes-left-out/), he understands that he needs to die for his peers to be able to kill the Dark Lord once and for all. Voldemort, predictably, uses the Avada Kedavra on Harry for a second time, and thinking him dead, makes Hagrid take his body to Hogwarts to celebrate his victory.

The explanation has to do with the logistics of Horcruxes, which not even Dumbledore, one of the brightest wizards ever born, fully understood, as he wasn't sure Harry would survive the killing curse for a second time. But he did.

## Harry's Survival Had Nothing to Do With the Resurrection Stone

### The Resurrection Stone's Ability to Bring the Dead Back Isn't That Simple

Harry Potter holds the Resurrection Stone in Deathly Hallows: Part 2.

Image via Warner Bros.

Many *Harry Potter* fans have speculated that [the Resurrection Stone's power](https://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-overpowered-magical-objects/) was able to help Harry survive the killing curse and return to living status. Except, that's not how the Resurrection Stone works. While it can "recall loved ones from the grave," **the *Harry Potter* universe's Resurrection Stone cannot fully restore someone to life**. As told in "The Tale of the Three Brothers," the Resurrection Stone brought the second brother's loved one back to the living world, but because she didn't belong there, she soon turned cold and sad.

The theory about Harry's survival was connected to his use of the Stone before his encounter with Voldemort in the Forbidden Forest. Recovering the Stone from the Golden Snitch, Harry recalled his mother and the Marauders for emotional support and then dropped it to the ground. As shown, he could see and hear his dead loved ones, but they remained incorporeal, and no one else could perceive them. Thus, bringing Harry back to life doesn't appear to be within the Stone's power.

## Why Voldemort Wasn't a Horcrux for Harry

### Horcruxes May Be a Mystery, But Fans Know Enough About Them to Recognize This Isn't Possible

Voldemort leads his followers into Hogwarts in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows - Part 2.

Image via Warner Bros.

Nobody knows exactly how [Horcruxes are made in *Harry Potter*](https://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-how-voldemort-horcruxes-destroyed/), as that information has never been fully divulged. While Voldemort used Harry's blood to regain his full body with dark magic, he could have become a Horcrux, of sorts. But the theory has some holes. To begin with, a Horcrux is connected to someone's soul, not their blood. Furthermore, to create a Horcrux, one has to first split one's soul by murdering someone, and Harry Potter never murdered anyone.

The killing curse is the worst spell a wizard can cast on another, and Voldemort creates Horcruxes by killing and storing pieces of his soul in objects and other living beings. Because **Harry did not kill anyone, he could not have made a Horcrux out of Voldemort**. Taking Harry's blood allowed Voldemort to negate the effects of Lily's protection over Harry and strengthened their connection, but unless a person's soul is in their blood, this would not make Voldemort a Horcrux.

Furthermore,[ if Voldemort had been Harry's Horcrux](https://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-horcruxes-facts/), Harry still wouldn't have come back to life in his original body. His soul would have been wandering until he had found a way to create a body of his own. When everyone thought Voldemort had died after the Avada Kedavra he aimed at baby Harry bounced back to him, he spent several years living as a sort of spirit and had to become a parasite in Quirinus Quirrell's body for a while.

## So, How Did Harry Potter Survive the Second Killing Curse?

### Voldemort's Killing Curse Struck the Remaining Piece of His Own Fractured Soul

Harry confronts Voldemort in the dark forest in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows Part 2.

Image via Warner Bros. 

The matter of surviving a killing curse is a full-circle moment for *Harry Potter*. By trying to kill him the first time, Voldemort bonds himself to Harry forever, with a small piece of his soul living inside him and marked by the scar. It explained [why Harry was able to speak Parseltongue](https://www.cbr.com/why-harry-potter-communicate-with-snakes/). Dumbledore was one of the few to realize this when he and Harry discovered that Voldemort had been using Horcruxes to cheat death in *The&#x20;**Half-Blood Prince*. Therefore, being one of the Horcruxes and a piece of his soul, Harry had to die to defeat evil — and as the unwitting creator, Voldemort himself would have to do it.

But Harry didn't die, and the reason is rather simple: **when Voldemort cast the killing curse, it only killed the piece of his soul within Harry, not Harry himself**. Ironically, this isn't the first time Voldemort's connection with Harry gave Harry the tools to avoid The Dark Lord's attacks. In this case, Harry survived Voldemort's curse because the villain had implanted a piece of his soul inside the young man, inadvertently creating a Horcrux.

This Horcrux also helped Harry during his second year at Hogwarts, when he could speak Parseltongue and get to the Chamber of Secrets to save Ginny Weasley. What's more, this connection allowed Harry to see things inside Voldemort's head and save Arthur Weasley's life. **By accidentally making Harry a Horcrux, Voldemort helps him time and time again, including enabling Harry to survive the Killing Curse** for the second time.

## What Does the Limbo Scene Represent in Harry Potter

### Harry Chooses Between Heading to the Afterlife or Coming Back to Life

Harry and Dumbledore talk after fighting Voldemort in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2.

Image via Warner Bros.

After Voldemort casts a second killing curse on Harry Potter, the Boy Who Lived is sent to some sort of limbo in *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2*. There, Harry sees Dumbledore in a version of King's Cross station, something that Dumbledore said was probably happening in his head. He also said that Harry could probably board a train if he wanted to, which presumably would've taken him to the afterlife.

What is curious about Harry seeing King's Cross station appearing in limbo is that **the real King's Cross station is itself a limbo between the Muggles and the Wizarding Worlds.** For Harry, the station has always been the place that separates his mundane reality in Little Whinging from the magic of Hogwarts. *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2* implies that limbo appears differently for every person. In life, the Hogwarts Express meant to Harry everything he ever cared for — his friends, his dreams, his future. There's a beautiful symbology behind Harry taking this same train to reach the afterlife, but this time, he must head in the opposite direction.

The big mystery surrounding the limbo scene from *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2&#x20;*&#x69;nvolves Dumbledore. Fans debate to this day whether Harry's soul truly encountered Dumbledore's or the Hogwarts headmaster's apparition was simply the fruit of Harry's imagination, as in a conversation with himself. It is a reasonable theory, but it's well known that when a wizard dies, they can choose between moving on or returning as a ghost. The second option often relates to unfinished business or fear of the afterlife. Presumably, limbo is where they make this choice, meaning that Dumbledore could've been waiting for Harry since he fell from the Astronomy Tower.

As for[ the bloody Voldemort baby Harry sees in limbo](https://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-scene-key-to-franchises-finale/), there's a more straightforward explanation: **since Voldemort only killed the piece of his soul stored within Harry, the baby represents this fragment of Voldemort's soul that was shredded by the villain's killing curse.&#x20;**&#x49;t seems like this second near-death experience brought Harry into limbo, but ultimately, Harry survived the curse. Harry Potter then decided to return to life to stop Voldemort. As such, perhaps Harry could've died at this moment, but he chose to come back to life.

## Harry Was Also Saved By Draco Malfoy's Mother, Narcissa

### Draco's Mother Saved Harry's Soul, But His Community Helped Him Survive Afterward

Narcissa Malfoy looks scared in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows.

Image via Warner Bros. 

Harry comes back to life after Voldemort's second killing curse thanks to the Horcrux inside of him, his mother's sacrifice, and Voldemort's hubris. However, if it wasn't for Narcissa Malfoy, it is unlikely — although never impossible when it comes to the Wizarding Word — that Harry would have survived a third Avada Kedavra. He was, after all, surrounded by Lord Voldemort and his Death Eaters, with no way to escape.

In the books,[ Lord Voldemort orders Narcissa Malfoy](https://www.cbr.com/harry-potter-death-eater-identities-explained/) to check if the Boy Who Lived has finally died, and in the movies, it is Bellatrix who asks her sister. Either way, in both versions, Narcissa gets close to Harry and checks his pulse. She notices Harry is alive, but she lies to Lord Voldemort, one of the most difficult things to do. Narcissa could have died if Voldemort had simply used legilimency on her. But once again, **Voldemort underestimated the strength of a person he considered weak and powerless**. He never saw Narcissa Malfoy lying to him, so Narcissa's one moment of goodness — out of worry about her own son, Draco — and Voldemort's great ego also played a role in Harry's survival.

What's more, it is interesting to consider that Voldemort sent someone else to check if Harry was alive. Voldemort went to war against the most powerful wizards and witches in magical society only to kill Harry Potter. He considered Harry his biggest threat from the moment he read the prophecy. At the same time, however, Voldemort considered himself above Harry's powers — and he was right. But when it came to checking Harry's pulse, Voldemort preferred another person to get close to him, instead of making sure for himself that [the Chosen One was finally dead](https://www.cbr.com/best-movies-chosen-one-trope/). Arguably, Voldemort was, in fact, afraid of Harry. Ultimately, Voldemort's fear led him to his death and destruction, much like his fear led him to create Harry as his antagonist.

## Did Harry Potter Die in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows?

### Harry Might Have Died For a Couple of Minutes, But Eventually Came Back To Life

Harry Potter casts Expelliarmus in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows: Part 2.

Image via Warner Bros.

While Harry survived the second killing curse Voldemort sent his way, the question remains whether he died even for a second and came back to life or had just passed out. The first time Voldemort [cast Avada Kedavra](https://www.cbr.com/what-avada-kedavra-mean-harry-potter/) against Harry, the curse didn't truly touch him. The magical impact left a mark on Harry, which might also be a product of Voldemort's soul entering his body as he unknowingly turned him into a Horcrux. However, Harry was never in danger.

**The second time the killing curse hits Harry, it is very different because it does impact the Boy Who Lived**. This time, it doesn't leave a mark (which might further confirm that Harry's scar comes from being a Horcrux and not from Avada Kedavra), but Harry at least passes out and has a spiritual experience. The Dumbledore in Harry's head (whether real or not) suggests that, since Harry was at a train station, free to choose his path, he could have decided to die or return. This is evidence that even though the Unforgivable Curse hit Voldemort's soul and not Harry's, the curse affected Harry's body this time.

If Harry was in a limbo space, choosing his next step and meeting a part of Dumbledore's soul, this could mean that his soul was outside his body. Harry had the choice of moving his soul back to his body, moving it to the afterlife, or maybe even becoming a ghost. If this scenario is true, Harry did die for a couple of minutes, up until the moment his soul presumably returns to his body and he comes back to life.

On the other hand, if Harry had imagined the whole situation, it would have meant he had been alive all that time. The impact of the Killing Curse on his body could have made him pass out, and he processed the moment through a dream.
